# Mahlanya RPG — Steam Store Listing

## Short Description (≤300 characters)

Play as Mahlanya — a daring young Swazi man — across 150 years of Eswatini history. Open-world UE5 RPG with geophysically accurate terrain, a cattle-based economy, and colonial resistance narratives. siSwati language throughout.

## About This Game (HTML, ~1500 words)

<p>
The Kingdom of Eswatini, 1750. The Dlamini dynasty is consolidating power across the Highveld, cattle kraals anchor every homestead, and the political order is maintained through ceremony, kinship, and the careful vocabulary of Inhlonipho. You are <strong>Mahlanya</strong> — meaning "the reckless one" in siSwati — a young man whose life will span four epochs of Swazi history, from pre-colonial formation to the last acts of colonial resistance in 1906. This is not a game about Africa as backdrop. It is a game about a specific place, a specific people, and a specific 156-year arc of sovereignty and survival.
</p>

<h2>The Land</h2>

<p>
Eswatini is not a uniform landscape. The Highveld granite escarpment in the west reaches elevations above 1,800 metres, its cliff faces weathered into blocky tors and seasonal waterfalls. The Middleveld rolls into gentler agricultural valleys where most Swazi settlement historically occurred. The Lowveld opens into acacia savanna, thorn scrub, and fever-tree corridors that shift character entirely with the season. The Lubombo plateau in the east is rhyolite — older, flatter, its escarpment a militarily significant ridge that defined the Battle of Lubombo in 1846.
</p>

<p>
Every terrain zone in Mahlanya RPG is derived from real GIS elevation data processed through a Zig SIMD erosion pipeline. Seasonal hydrology shifts river crossings and trail conditions. A cattle herd grazing the Lowveld in the dry months will churn the soil; the same area during the rains becomes a navigational obstacle. Weather is simulated, not cosmetic.
</p>

<h2>The Economy</h2>

<p>
Cattle are not inventory items. In Mahlanya RPG, cattle are the foundation of the political economy — they function as currency, as social capital, and as spiritual resource. Lobola (cattle paid at marriage) establishes kinship alliances. Tribute in cattle signals political submission or goodwill between chiefdoms. Raiding cattle from a rival polity is an act of war or of demonstration. Your herd's size determines what diplomatic options are available to you, which missions you can take, and how Inkosi (chiefs) and elders respond when you arrive at their Umuti (homestead).
</p>

<p>
The Sibaya — the cattle kraal at the centre of every homestead — is the literal and symbolic heart of the game economy. Cattle health, breed quality, and herd demographics (bulls, cows, calves) all affect value. Drought reduces herd condition. Raids can deplete you. Sound management of your cattle, and strategic use of them in negotiations, is as important as combat competence.
</p>

<h2>The Protocols</h2>

<p>
Swazi society operates through layered systems of respect and obligation. <strong>Inhlonipho</strong> — the avoidance vocabulary and respect code — governs how you address the Inkosi, how you greet elders, and what you can say and to whom. The game enforces this: dialogue options with a senior indvuna (headman) are not the same as dialogue options with a peer. Approaching an elder without the correct greeting reduces your standing; it can close quest branches or trigger consequences.
</p>

<p>
The game is voiced and subtitled in <strong>siSwati</strong>, the national language of Eswatini, with English glosses for all terminology. Cultural ceremonies — the <strong>Incwala</strong> (first fruits kingship ceremony) and the <strong>Umhlanga</strong> (Reed Dance) — are depicted with the accuracy that comes from documented historical sources and cultural consultation. They are not mini-games. They are events that change the simulation state: political tensions shift, NPC relationships update, and historical triggers activate.
</p>

<h2>The History</h2>

<p>
Mahlanya's life spans four historical eras, each with its own political pressures and narrative possibilities:
</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Pre-colonial formation (~1750):</strong> The Dlamini kingdom consolidating across highland and valley. Diplomatic missions, tribute negotiations, inter-chiefdom rivalry.</li>
  <li><strong>Mfecane upheaval (1815–1840):</strong> The regional disruption driven by Zulu expansion and the displacement of multiple polities creates refugee crises, raid economies, and shifting alliance structures across southern Africa. The Swazi state is tested and reformed.</li>
  <li><strong>Battle of Lubombo (1846):</strong> A historically documented military engagement on the eastern escarpment. Players experience this as a playable battle with period-accurate weapons, terrain, and tactical stakes — not a cutscene.</li>
  <li><strong>Colonial resistance (1880–1906):</strong> The concession crisis, the Ngwenyama's increasingly constrained sovereignty, and the formal annexation of Swaziland by Britain in 1903. The political economy collapses under debt instruments and land alienation. Mahlanya navigates the end of an era.</li>
</ul>

<p>
Narrative is emergent. A knowledge graph enforces historical accuracy: no NPC can assert a historically false claim. If drought conditions are simulated and cattle raid probability rises, related quests generate. Colonial pressure in the late eras increases concession-related crisis events. The simulation drives the story.
</p>

<h2>Co-op</h2>

<p>
Two to four players can take on cattle raid missions and diplomatic escort assignments together. Co-op sessions are structured around historically plausible scenarios: escorting lobola cattle to a distant chiefdom, defending a homestead from a simulated raid, or navigating a joint diplomatic mission to the Ngwenyama's court. Co-op does not break cultural protocols — all players are subject to the same Inhlonipho rules. Your party's collective cattle holdings and standing determine what options are available.
</p>

<h2>The Pipeline</h2>

<p>
Mahlanya RPG is built on Unreal Engine 5 with Nanite geometry and Lumen global illumination. The terrain is generated from real GIS data using a custom Zig SIMD erosion pipeline, producing heightfields that match the actual topographic character of each zone. Audio uses 64-ray geometric propagation at Ultra settings. Settlement placement uses Voronoi partitioning seeded from historical population distribution data. Ten simulation plugins govern the game state at runtime: weather, cattle health, political pressure, seasonal economy, historical triggers, and more.
</p>

<p>
PC is the primary platform. A pre-baked mobile tier (iOS and Android) uses the same world geometry at reduced fidelity with hardware-adaptive scaling. A console phase is planned post-launch.
</p>

</p>

## Developer Note

Mahlanya RPG exists because I grew up in Manzini and there are no games set here — not in Eswatini, not in Swazi history, not in a world where the Sibaya is the centre of the economy and Inhlonipho is the grammar of human relationships. I am Charles Bartaria, the developer at BRT Inc., and this game is built in the country it depicts. The history in this game is not flavour text: the Mfecane disruptions, the concession crisis, the Battle of Lubombo — these are documented events, and we have handled them with the rigour they deserve. Eswatini has a history worth playing. This is that game.
